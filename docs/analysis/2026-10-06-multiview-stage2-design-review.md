## Purpose, implemented behavior and proposed architecture

The service provides scoped OWNER access to owned local recorders through a Windows native bridge, Python media/SQL services and the React/Tailwind monitoring wall. The user target is twelve simultaneous cameras across eight plus four channels, substream grid and one main focus view.

Stage 1 currently uses exclusive native sessions per channel. Its independently recorded c12 evidence establishes nine streams advancing over a common five-minute window and normal retirement with complete bounded observation. It does not establish twelve simultaneous streams, a universal recorder login cap, ten successful main opens or stage-2 dispatch safety. Those limitations remain intact.

Stage 2 proposes two actor/session-scoped recorder originals, immutable sub tasks per requested channel, separately authorized parked retention, fresh local viewer/decoder registrations and one dedicated original main lane. Native ownership and cleanup move to durable epoch/slot rows; viewer cleanup becomes explicitly typed. Shared pressure and memory limits, additive SQL migration and exact original custody are proposed work, not implemented or executed outcomes.

## Important corrections before approval

### I1 — Promised revocation timing exceeds the inherited authority contract

**Spec lines 208–213 and 409–410.** The acceptance text promises individual/global revocation within the existing 100 ms gate and 200 ms emission bounds, including delayed SQL. The inherited 100 ms value is a call-entry observation-age contract. It is not an end-to-end denial or return-time freshness deadline.

Evidence: `local_media_admission.py:418–425` permits an eligible waiter to reuse its in-flight result; `:440–460` anchors ordinary cache expiry to query start but returns a successful result without a return-time expiry check. The accepted R120/R133 review explicitly permits slow success after expiry (`docs/analysis/2026-10-05-multiview-stage1-native-review.md:54,240,332,341`). The current WebRTC guide (`docs/integrations/tvt-local-webrtc.md:409–432`) separates that call-entry bound from clipped sender permits and monotonic clock granularity. A completed late result can authorize its eligible invocation even though ordinary reusable cache lifetime is already over. Reusing this contract cannot prove the blanket 100 ms end-to-end assertion.

**Required correction:** state precisely that inherited authority reuse is start-anchored and eligible slow success remains as accepted; distinguish immediate local sticky denial after observation from the time at which SQL revocation is observed. Keep sender permits independently anchored/clipped and state their clock-tick qualification. If stage 2 requires a stronger return-time or absolute revocation deadline, explicitly propose the additional local expiry/late-result mechanism and its compatibility with R133 as a design change. Do not claim that stronger mechanism already exists. Master/slot grants also need expiry checks before transport renewal; another channel's success must not refresh them.

**Finite proof criterion:** use a controllable delayed SQL answer that completes beyond freshness and separately beyond lease expiry, plus denial racing with that answer. Assert the specified eligible-call result, no renewed reusable cache interval, no expired sender permit, and no revival after local denial. Report observation, call-entry, return and sender cut-off as separate boundaries. A 100 ms end-to-end result must not be the expected inherited outcome.

### I2 — A validated-picture watermark does not fence pre-admission buffered bytes

**Spec lines 140–141 and 170–184, with proof lines 376–380.** Clearing queued units/RGB and recording the last validated slot ordinal can leave an older key picture in the original callback queue, IPC envelope or shared partial fragment assembler. If that picture is validated only after attachment, it obtains a later ordinal and can satisfy the stated watermark. A key picture partially received before admission and completed afterward is another case. This conflicts with the prohibition on retained pre-lease frames even though camera capture-time proof is correctly excluded.

The existing parent pump explicitly has outstanding IPC envelopes and a separate validated-frame queue (`local_live_ipc.py:287–300`). The wire reference documents partial frames/fragments (`docs/integrations/tvt-local-live-codec.md`, Receive subset). These are real buffering boundaries to account for in the new architecture; this review did not claim an implemented stage-2 exploit.

**Required correction:** define the attachment cut at a serialized original-ingress boundary and preserve enough bounded provenance through callback, IPC, assembly and dispatch to exclude retained pre-cut bytes from the new viewer's first key/parameter sets. An equivalent bounded complete-message barrier is acceptable. Shared buffered messages for other active slots need not be discarded; this rule filters eligibility for the attaching viewer and does not synthesize a native task fence. Specify how a fragment crossing the cut is excluded. Keep the explicit distinction between application-retained material and unknowable camera capture time.

**Finite proof criterion:** hold a complete key in callback/IPC, hold a partial key/parameter-set envelope in assembly, and hold a returned-but-uncommitted picture before admission. Release each after the new registration. None may seed its decoder/publication or new proof. A genuinely post-cut complete key may start it within the existing 10 s/caller budget. Sibling slots retain correct ordering, credit accounting and progress. Every delayed decoder result/publication also needs the exact new registration generation, so an old completion cannot cross the cut.

### I3 — Focus reservation has an undefined authority gap at last-viewer transitions

**Spec lines 118–125, 188–203 and 293–305.** Focus first closes the sub viewer, then issues main; return first closes/reaps main, then issues sub. The reservation is described as tied to the *current main viewer* and expiring with it. When the sub viewer is the recorder's only active device reference, no main viewer exists yet during the forward handoff. On return, that main viewer has already closed. The last-reference rule therefore retires the base master at either gap unless a separately valid transition reservation exists. That conflicts with the intended same-parked-slot return, and retaining it without such a grant would conflict with the last-owner rule.

**Required correction:** specify a bounded transition reservation admitted atomically before removing the last current viewer reference, under current same-actor/channel/wall authority. Give its start/return states, expiry and cancellation/failure settlement. Main issuance still follows sub CLOSED, and sub issuance still follows main CLOSED plus exact original reap. The reservation must cover the handoff interval without being a second viewer or a new publication grant. Transfer/consume it atomically on successful attachment; failed/BUSY/cancelled/expired handoff releases it and triggers last-owner retirement if no other valid reference exists. Alternatively explicitly choose full-master retirement for the sole-viewer case and describe its changed return behavior.

**Finite proof criterion:** run the sole-active-viewer focus and return state transitions, cancelling before and after each local/native barrier. Verify no authority-free master retention, no premature base shutdown under a valid reservation, no second active channel viewer, and no replacement admission before uncertain original reap. A reservation has a numeric maximum clipped to original caller and wall/channel deadlines; repeated clicks cannot renew it indefinitely.

## Minor clarification

### M1 — Assign proof gates to stages and make the rapid-switch claim measurable

**Spec lines 367–419.** The proof catalogue is useful and its unresolved actual-device questions are appropriately gated. Its heading combines pre-implementation and acceptance obligations even though inert/native probes require reviewed implementation. Also, ten rapid switches currently says only that bounds and uniqueness survive; c12 already demonstrates why ten click pairs cannot imply ten successful main opens.

Clarify that APK/source review closes the architectural evidence gate; the implementation plan defines finite inert/SQL/custody checks; reviewed owned probes then progress two/four tasks before the final eight-plus-four acceptance. Keep all stages subject to existing caller/cleanup budgets and a bounded run deadline. Define one fully presented main-to-sub return as a functional success, and ten rapid requests as a separate cancellation/uniqueness test with reported attempted, acknowledged, presented, cancelled and settled counts. Require measured sibling progress across the completed handoff. Unknown actual protocol behavior remains a probe gate rather than an invented pre-approval blocker.

## Accepted design boundaries and remaining risk

- Taskless route0 and channel-based `ep3.e` association are correctly distinguished from native task incarnation. Current parser source (`local_live.py:487–510`) supports the existing exclusive-first-task restriction and task-bearing route2 checks. The proposed one-immutable-task-per-channel epoch assertion requires its own proof; it does not reuse exclusive-first-task by simply disabling it.
- Pending command/sequence correlation, original transport generations, refusals and same-session permission evidence remain required. No presumed close ACK, ignored routing field or local viewer generation is promoted into a wire fence.
- Parking is an explicit product decision with ongoing network/device cost, charged slots, bounded separately renewable rights and whole-master retirement upon retention-authority loss. Full-recorder interruption is honest and acceptable if approved. It is not proof that physical traffic ceases immediately when local publication is denied.
- Dedicated main respects channel uniqueness and an extra possible BUSY login. Exact dedicated original reap before return, typed viewer/epoch cleanup and unchanged legacy-row semantics are appropriate. Current whole-session cleanup source (`local_media_service.py:268–294`) confirms that a new typed contract really is required.
- Epoch STOPPING blocks late attachment. Original Popen/Job/IPC/thread ownership, charged quarantine and conservative global uncertain-owner fencing are preserved. Worker-crash recovery is explicitly outside this version; SQL cannot manufacture actual native retirement.
- Per-slot credit, global encoded budget, retained output/conversion accounting, finite shared receive pressure and proposed process/Job caps are concrete architectural constraints. Their ability to sustain twelve channels and Windows Job compatibility are genuine proof obligations. The 9.5 GiB sum is explicitly a proposed ceiling, not measured load or a claim that this host qualifies. No extra sizing blocker is invented from missing out-of-scope execution.
- The additive migration must implement master/slot/viewer authority independently, preserve RLS/current checks and count parked/uncertain resources. A master True must never become an all-channel grant. Accepted R133 process-lifetime denied-entry growth remains historical; new grants must not silently imply that all metadata is bounded by media-byte credits.

Open user decisions remain parking semantics, whole-recorder interruption on retention revocation, the extra main login/BUSY case, single OWNER/wall scope and memory/admission profile. They should be approved only after the important spec corrections are reviewed. No implementation plan or product-code work is authorized by this review.
